1. Glow-in-the-dark stickers are an example of fluorescence. (True or False)
Options: True, False
Correct Answer: False
Reason: Glow-in-the-dark materials are an example of phosphorescence (slow delayed emission), not fluorescence (instantaneous emission).

2. Explain why every element produces its own unique line spectrum, and how astronomers use this to find out which elements are present in a distant star. (Essay)
Correct Answer: Each element has unique electron energy levels, emitting or absorbing specific wavelengths of light (a spectral fingerprint). Astronomers compare the lines in a star's spectrum against known laboratory spectra of elements to identify which elements are present in the star.

3. As the electron in hydrogen moves to higher values of n, which quantity INCREASES? (Multiple Choice)
Options:
A. Kinetic energy
B. Size of the potential energy
C. Speed
D. Total energy (it becomes less negative)
Correct Answer: D. Total energy (it becomes less negative)
Reason: As n increases, orbital speed and kinetic energy decrease, while total energy En = -13.6/n² eV becomes less negative and increases toward 0.

4. Find the wavelength (in nm) of the first line of the Lyman series (n = 2 -> 1) of hydrogen. (Numeric)
Correct Answer: 121.6
Reason: Using 1/λ = R(1 - 1/4) = 3R/4, with R ≈ 1.097 × 10⁷ m⁻¹, λ ≈ 121.6 nm.

5. The 2s orbital is smaller than the 1s orbital. (True or False)
Options: True, False
Correct Answer: False
Reason: The 2s orbital corresponds to n = 2 and is larger in size and higher in energy than the 1s orbital (n = 1).

7. Find the energy of the electron in the n = 4 level of He+ (Z = 2), in eV (include the sign). (Numeric)
Correct Answer: -3.4
Reason: En = -13.6 × (Z² / n²) eV = -13.6 × (4 / 16) = -3.4 eV.

8. Why do the electrons in the gold atoms have almost no effect on the paths of the alpha particles? (Multiple Choice)
Options:
A. Electrons move too slowly to meet the alpha particles
B. Electrons are far too light to deflect the much heavier alpha particles
C. Electrons have no charge
D. Electrons are inside the nucleus
Correct Answer: B. Electrons are far too light to deflect the much heavier alpha particles
Reason: Alpha particles are roughly 7300 times heavier than electrons, so collisions with electrons do not noticeably deflect them.

9. Which element was discovered in the spectrum of the Sun before it was found on Earth? (Multiple Choice)
Options:
A. Neon
B. Sodium
C. Hydrogen
D. Helium
Correct Answer: D. Helium
Reason: Helium was discovered in 1868 during a solar eclipse as an unknown yellow spectral line in the Sun's chromosphere.

10. The energy levels of hydrogen get closer together as n increases. (True or False)
Options: True, False
Correct Answer: True
Reason: The energy gap ΔE = 13.6(1/n² - 1/(n+1)²) decreases as n grows larger, converging toward 0 eV at n = ∞.

12. Which equation is Bohr's quantum condition for the allowed orbits? (Multiple Choice)
Options:
A. mv²/r = kZe²/r²
B. mvr = nh/(2π)
C. E = mc²
D. λ = h/(mv)
Correct Answer: B. mvr = nh/(2π)
Reason: Bohr postulated that angular momentum is quantised in integer multiples of h/(2π): L = mvr = nh/(2π).

14. A 1 mW laser pointer can permanently damage the eye, but looking briefly at a 60 W light bulb does not. Using the properties of laser light, explain why. Include a calculation or estimate to support your argument. (Essay)
Correct Answer: A laser emits a coherent, parallel beam that the eye's lens focuses onto a tiny retinal spot (A ≈ 10⁻⁸ m²), creating a dangerous intensity (I = P/A = 0.001 W / 10⁻⁸ m² = 10⁵ W/m²). A 60 W bulb emits incoherent light in all directions, so at 1 m away its intensity spreads over a sphere (A = 4πr² ≈ 12.6 m²), yielding only I ≈ 4.8 W/m².

15. What is the shape of an s orbital? (Multiple Choice)
Options:
A. A four-leaf clover
B. A sphere
C. A dumbbell
D. A flat ring
Correct Answer: B. A sphere
Reason: An s orbital has l = 0, which has spherical symmetry.

16. Which sub-shell is filled immediately after 4s? (Multiple Choice)
Options:
A. 3d
B. 4d
C. 5s
D. 4p
Correct Answer: A. 3d
Reason: According to the Aufbau principle (n + l rule), after 4s (n+l = 4), electrons fill 3d (n+l = 5).

17. Which photon can be absorbed by the hydrogen atom to go to an excited state. (Multiple Choice)
Options:
A. Photon of energy 7.2 eV
B. Photon of energy 20.1 eV
C. Photon of energy 13.1 eV
D. Photon of energy 10.2 eV
Correct Answer: D. Photon of energy 10.2 eV
Reason: Transition from ground state n = 1 (-13.6 eV) to n = 2 (-3.4 eV) requires exactly ΔE = 10.2 eV.

20. For n = 3, which values of l are allowed? (Multiple Choice)
Options:
A. 0, 1, 2, 3
B. 1, 2, 3
C. 0 only
D. 0, 1, 2
Correct Answer: D. 0, 1, 2
Reason: For any principal quantum number n, l takes integer values from 0 up to n - 1. For n = 3: 0, 1, 2.

21. An electron with 11.0 eV of kinetic energy collides with a ground-state hydrogen atom. What can happen? (Multiple Choice)
Options:
A. Nothing, because 11.0 eV matches no gap
B. It can excite the atom to n = 2 and keep 0.8 eV
C. It must ionise the atom
D. It can excite the atom to n = 3
Correct Answer: B. It can excite the atom to n = 2 and keep 0.8 eV
Reason: Colliding particles can transfer part of their kinetic energy. Excitations to n = 2 requires 10.2 eV; the electron keeps 11.0 - 10.2 = 0.8 eV.

22. A p sub-shell contains three orbitals. (True or False)
Options: True, False
Correct Answer: True
Reason: For l = 1 (p sub-shell), ml = -1, 0, +1 (three orbitals: px, py, pz).

23. Ground-state hydrogen is bombarded with electrons of 12.8 eV. Find the LONGEST wavelength of light (in nm) that the gas can then emit. (Numeric)
Correct Answer: 656.1 (or 656)
Reason: 12.8 eV can excite atoms up to n = 4 (requires 12.75 eV). De-excitation includes n = 3 -> 2 emitting H-alpha at 656.1 nm.

24. Explain why light from an ordinary bulb is incoherent while laser light is coherent. Refer to spontaneous and stimulated emission in your answer. (Essay)
Correct Answer: An ordinary bulb produces light via spontaneous emission, where excited atoms release photons independently at random times, directions, phases, and wavelengths (incoherent). A laser uses stimulated emission, where an incoming photon triggers an excited atom to emit an identical photon with the exact same wavelength, phase, and direction (coherent).

25. What is the maximum number of electrons that the n = 4 shell can hold? (Numeric)
Correct Answer: 32
Reason: Max electrons in shell n is 2n² = 2(4²) = 32.

26. If the speed of an electron doubles, its de Broglie wavelength halves. (True or False)
Options: True, False
Correct Answer: True
Reason: λ = h / (mv); wavelength is inversely proportional to speed.

27. Which set of quantum numbers (n, l, ml, ms) is NOT allowed? (Multiple Choice)
Options:
A. (2, 2, 0, +1/2)
B. (2, 1, -1, +1/2)
C. (3, 2, -2, -1/2)
D. (1, 0, 0, -1/2)
Correct Answer: A. (2, 2, 0, +1/2)
Reason: The orbital quantum number l must satisfy 0 <= l <= n - 1. When n = 2, l can only be 0 or 1.

28. Why was the gold-foil apparatus kept in a vacuum? (Multiple Choice)
Options:
A. So that air molecules would not stop or scatter the alpha particles
B. So that the alpha particles would travel faster than light
C. So that the gold foil would not melt
D. So that the electrons could leave the gold atoms
Correct Answer: A. So that air molecules would not stop or scatter the alpha particles
Reason: Alpha particles have low penetration and would easily be stopped or scattered by collisions with air molecules.

29. Two lasers have the same power. Laser A emits 400 nm light and laser B emits 800 nm light. Which statement is correct? (Multiple Choice)
Options:
A. Both emit the same number of photons per second
B. Laser A emits four times as many photons per second
C. Laser B emits twice as many photons per second as laser A
D. Laser A emits twice as many photons per second as laser B
Correct Answer: C. Laser B emits twice as many photons per second as laser A
Reason: Photon energy E = hc/λ. Each 800 nm photon carries half as much energy as a 400 nm photon, so laser B needs twice as many photons per second to reach the same power.

Options:

B. About 10⁻⁵ m
C. About 10⁻¹⁵ m

Correct Answer: D. About 10⁻¹⁰ m

33. How much energy is needed to ionise a hydrogen atom that is already in the n = 2 level? (Multiple Choice)
Options:
A. 13.6 eV
B. 10.2 eV
C. 3.4 eV
D. 1.51 eV
Correct Answer: C. 3.4 eV
Reason: At n = 2, electron energy is -13.6/2² = -3.4 eV. Ionisation requires reaching 0 eV: 0 - (-3.4) = 3.4 eV.
