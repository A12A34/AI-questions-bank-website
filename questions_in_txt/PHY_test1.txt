Part 1 — 50 Multiple-Choice Questions
1. What is light?
A. A type of sound wave
B. A form of energy and an electromagnetic wave
C. A mechanical wave
D. A type of matter  
Answer: B
Reason: Light is electromagnetic radiation that carries energy.
2. What does light need to travel through empty space?
A. Air
B. Water
C. A material medium
D. Nothing  
Answer: D
Reason: Electromagnetic waves can travel through a vacuum.
3. What is the speed of light in vacuum?
A. 3 × 10^6 m/s
B. 3 × 10^7 m/s
C. 3 × 10^8 m/s
D. 3 × 10^9 m/s  
Answer: C
Reason: The speed of light is approximately 3 × 10^8 m/s.
4. Which symbol represents the speed of light?
A. h
B. c
C. f
D. λ  
Answer: B
Reason: c represents the speed of light in vacuum.
5. Which equation relates speed, frequency, and wavelength?
A. c = fλ
B. E = mc²
C. E = hf
D. p = mv  
Answer: A
Reason: The wave equation is c = fλ.
6. What is wavelength?
A. Number of waves per second
B. Maximum height of a wave
C. Distance between neighbouring crests
D. Speed of a wave  
Answer: C
Reason: Wavelength is the distance from one crest to the next.
7. What is frequency measured in?
A. Metres
B. Joules
C. Hertz
D. Watts  
Answer: C
Reason: Frequency is measured in hertz (Hz), meaning cycles per second.
8. What does a larger amplitude of visible light generally mean?
A. Lower speed
B. Brighter light
C. Higher frequency
D. Longer wavelength  
Answer: B
Reason: Greater amplitude corresponds to greater brightness.
9. Light is classified as what type of wave?
A. Longitudinal
B. Transverse
C. Mechanical
D. Sound  
Answer: B
Reason: The electric and magnetic fields vibrate perpendicular to the direction of travel.
10. In an electromagnetic wave, the electric and magnetic fields are:
A. Parallel to each other
B. At right angles to each other
C. In the same direction as travel
D. Not related  
Answer: B
Reason: The electric and magnetic fields are perpendicular to each other and to the direction of travel.
11. Which phenomenon is best explained by the ray model?
A. Diffraction
B. Interference
C. Reflection
D. Polarisation  
Answer: C
Reason: Reflection can be described using rays.
12. What is the law of reflection?
A. i + r = 90°
B. i = r
C. i = 2r
D. i = r/2  
Answer: B
Reason: The angle of incidence equals the angle of reflection.
13. What causes refraction?
A. Light disappears
B. Light changes speed between media
C. Light becomes sound
D. Light stops vibrating  
Answer: B
Reason: Light bends because its speed changes when it enters a different medium.
14. Which equation gives refractive index?
A. n = v/c
B. n = c/v
C. n = cv
D. n = c + v  
Answer: B
Reason: n = c/v.
15. When light enters glass from air, its frequency:
A. Increases
B. Decreases
C. Stays the same
D. Becomes zero  
Answer: C
Reason: Frequency does not change when light enters another medium.
16. When light enters glass from air, its wavelength generally:
A. Increases
B. Decreases
C. Stays exactly the same
D. Becomes infinite  
Answer: B
Reason: The speed decreases while frequency stays constant, so wavelength decreases.
17. Which phenomenon separates white light into different colours?
A. Reflection
B. Polarisation
C. Dispersion
D. Diffraction  
Answer: C
Reason: Dispersion occurs because different wavelengths bend by different amounts.
18. Which phenomenon produces bright and dark fringes?
A. Interference
B. Reflection
C. Refraction
D. Absorption  
Answer: A
Reason: Interference produces regions of constructive and destructive interference.
19. Diffraction is the:
A. Absorption of light
B. Spreading of waves around edges or through narrow gaps
C. Reflection of light
D. Conversion of light to heat  
Answer: B
Reason: Diffraction causes waves to spread after passing through small openings or around obstacles.
20. What does polarisation show about light?
A. Light is longitudinal
B. Light is transverse
C. Light has no energy
D. Light needs air  
Answer: B
Reason: Only transverse waves can be polarised.
21. Which is the correct order of the electromagnetic spectrum from lowest to highest frequency?
A. Gamma, X-ray, UV, visible, IR, microwave, radio
B. Radio, microwave, IR, visible, UV, X-ray, gamma
C. Radio, IR, microwave, visible, X-ray, UV, gamma
D. Visible, radio, microwave, IR, UV, gamma, X-ray  
Answer: B
Reason: Frequency increases from radio waves toward gamma rays.
22. Which electromagnetic wave has the longest wavelength?
A. Gamma
B. X-ray
C. Radio
D. UV  
Answer: C
Reason: Radio waves have the longest wavelengths.
23. Which electromagnetic wave has the highest frequency?
A. Radio
B. Visible
C. X-ray
D. Gamma  
Answer: D
Reason: Gamma rays have the highest frequencies.
24. Which has the greatest photon energy?
A. Radio
B. Infrared
C. Visible light
D. Gamma rays  
Answer: D
Reason: Photon energy increases with frequency: E = hf.
25. Which visible colour has greater photon energy?
A. Red
B. Orange
C. Blue
D. Red and blue have the same energy  
Answer: C
Reason: Blue has a higher frequency and therefore greater photon energy.
26. What is the photon energy equation?
A. E = hf
B. E = h/f
C. E = f/h
D. E = mc²  
Answer: A
Reason: Photon energy is E = hf.
27. Which equation can calculate photon energy using wavelength in nm?
A. E = 1240λ
B. E = 1240/λ
C. E = λ/1240
D. E = 1240 + λ  
Answer: B
Reason: E(eV) = 1240/λ(nm).
28. What is a photon?
A. A packet of light energy
B. A proton
C. A neutron
D. A sound wave  
Answer: A
Reason: A photon is a discrete packet of electromagnetic energy.
29. What is a blackbody?
A. An object that reflects all radiation
B. An ideal object that absorbs all incident radiation
C. An object that emits only visible light
D. An object with no temperature  
Answer: B
Reason: A blackbody absorbs all radiation falling on it.
30. What determines the radiation emitted by an ideal blackbody?
A. Its colour only
B. Its material only
C. Its temperature
D. Its shape only  
Answer: C
Reason: Blackbody radiation depends only on temperature.
31. As the temperature of a blackbody increases, the peak wavelength:
A. Gets longer
B. Gets shorter
C. Does not change
D. Becomes zero  
Answer: B
Reason: Hotter objects peak at shorter wavelengths.
32. Which object is approximately a blackbody?
A. The Sun
B. A mirror
C. A transparent window
D. A cold piece of glass  
Answer: A
Reason: The Sun is a good approximate blackbody.
33. A hotter star generally appears:
A. More red
B. More blue-white
C. Completely black
D. Invisible  
Answer: B
Reason: Higher temperatures shift the peak toward shorter, bluer wavelengths.
34. What was the ultraviolet catastrophe?
A. Light disappearing in ultraviolet
B. Classical theory predicting unlimited energy at short wavelengths
C. UV light being unable to travel
D. UV light becoming sound  
Answer: B
Reason: Classical physics predicted increasing energy toward very short wavelengths, contrary to experiment.
35. Who introduced the idea that energy is emitted in discrete packets?
A. Newton
B. Planck
C. Hertz
D. Young  
Answer: B
Reason: Planck proposed energy quanta to explain blackbody radiation.
36. What does E = hf represent in blackbody/quantum theory?
A. Photon energy
B. Electron momentum
C. Wave speed
D. Refractive index  
Answer: A
Reason: Energy is quantised in packets with energy hf.
37. What is the photoelectric effect?
A. Light bending in glass
B. Light knocking electrons out of a metal
C. Light splitting into colours
D. Electrons producing sound  
Answer: B
Reason: The photoelectric effect is the emission of electrons from a metal due to light.
38. What determines the maximum kinetic energy of photoelectrons?
A. Light intensity only
B. Frequency of the light
C. Distance from the metal
D. Metal colour only  
Answer: B
Reason: KEmax = hf − φ, so frequency determines photon energy and electron kinetic energy.
39. What determines how many photoelectrons are emitted?
A. Intensity, when frequency is above threshold
B. Frequency only
C. Work function only
D. Wavelength only  
Answer: A
Reason: Greater intensity means more photons and therefore more emitted electrons.
40. What is the work function?
A. Maximum electron energy
B. Minimum energy needed to remove an electron from a metal
C. Energy of a photon after emission
D. Speed of an electron  
Answer: B
Reason: The work function φ is the minimum energy needed to free an electron.
41. What is threshold frequency?
A. The highest possible frequency
B. The minimum frequency needed to eject electrons
C. The frequency of visible light only
D. The frequency of sound  
Answer: B
Reason: Below the threshold frequency, photoemission does not occur.
42. Which equation represents the photoelectric effect?
A. KEmax = hf − φ
B. KEmax = hf + φ
C. KEmax = φ − hf
D. KEmax = h/f  
Answer: A
Reason: Photon energy first supplies the work function; the remainder becomes kinetic energy.
43. What is stopping potential?
A. Voltage that accelerates the fastest electrons
B. Reverse voltage just sufficient to stop the fastest photoelectrons
C. Voltage across a resistor
D. Voltage needed to create photons  
Answer: B
Reason: The stopping potential prevents even the fastest photoelectrons from reaching the collector.
44. What happens if light frequency is below the threshold frequency?
A. More electrons are emitted if brightness increases
B. Electrons eventually come out
C. No photoelectrons are emitted
D. Electrons become faster  
Answer: C
Reason: Each photon lacks enough energy to overcome the work function.
45. What did de Broglie propose?
A. Only light can behave as a wave
B. Matter particles can also behave as waves
C. Electrons have no mass
D. Light has no energy  
Answer: B
Reason: de Broglie proposed matter waves with λ = h/p.
46. What is the de Broglie wavelength equation?
A. λ = hp
B. λ = h/p
C. λ = p/h
D. λ = hf  
Answer: B
Reason: Matter-wave wavelength is inversely proportional to momentum.
47. If a particle's momentum increases, its de Broglie wavelength:
A. Increases
B. Decreases
C. Stays the same
D. Becomes infinite  
Answer: B
Reason: λ = h/p.
48. Which experiment demonstrates electron diffraction?
A. Young's experiment
B. Davisson-Germer experiment
C. Photoelectric experiment
D. Millikan experiment  
Answer: B
Reason: Davisson-Germer demonstrated electron diffraction. The study guide marks this experiment as NOT in the exam.
49. Which is evidence that light behaves as a wave?
A. Photoelectric effect
B. Blackbody radiation
C. Interference
D. Photon absorption  
Answer: C
Reason: Interference is a wave phenomenon.
50. Which statement best describes wave-particle duality?
A. Light is always only a wave
B. Matter is always only a particle
C. Both light and matter can show wave and particle behaviour
D. Waves and particles are completely unrelated  
Answer: C
Reason: Experiments show both light and matter have wave-like and particle-like behaviour.

Reason: Electromagnetic waves do not require a material medium.

Answer: False
Reason: Sound needs a medium, but light can travel through vacuum.

Answer: True
Reason: This is the value of c.
54. Light is a longitudinal wave.
Answer: False
Reason: Light is a transverse electromagnetic wave.
55. The electric and magnetic fields of an electromagnetic wave are perpendicular to each other.
Answer: True
Reason: They oscillate at right angles to each other.
56. Frequency is measured in metres.
Answer: False
Reason: Frequency is measured in hertz.
57. Wavelength is measured in metres.
Answer: True
Reason: The SI unit of wavelength is metre.
58. The amplitude of visible light is related to its brightness.
Answer: True
Reason: Greater amplitude corresponds to brighter light.
59. In vacuum, all electromagnetic waves travel at the same speed.
Answer: True
Reason: All EM radiation travels at 3 × 10^8 m/s in vacuum.
60. Radio waves have a shorter wavelength than gamma rays.
Answer: False
Reason: Radio waves have the longest wavelengths.
61. Gamma rays have the highest frequency in the electromagnetic spectrum.
Answer: True
Reason: Gamma rays are at the highest-frequency end.

Answer: False
Reason: Infrared has lower frequency than visible light.

101. A light wave has a wavelength of 600 nm. What is its frequency?
A. 5 × 10^12 Hz
B. 5 × 10^14 Hz
C. 2 × 10^14 Hz
D. 1.8 × 10^17 Hz
Answer: B
Reason:
λ = 600 nm = 6 × 10^-7 m
f = c/λ
f = (3 × 10^8)/(6 × 10^-7) = 5 × 10^14 Hz.
102. What is the wavelength of a 100 MHz radio wave?
A. 0.3 m
B. 3 m
C. 30 m
D. 300 m
Answer: B
Reason:
100 MHz = 1 × 10^8 Hz
λ = c/f = (3 × 10^8)/(1 × 10^8) = 3 m.
103. A light wave has a frequency of 6 × 10^14 Hz. What is its wavelength?
A. 50 nm
B. 500 nm
C. 5000 nm
D. 0.5 nm
Answer: B
Reason:
λ = c/f
